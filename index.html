What Your Organisation Needs

Every organization is different, but every organization shares foundational needs. The system aims to provide the foundational needs of all organizations.

  • Aims, plans and policies: The first steps in creating your new organization are to publish your organisational aims, plans, and policies. Everyone has direct access to these when using the system. It is a way to keep the members informed and on track.
  • Membership: Store and manage member information in a database.
  • Automated onboarding: The new person sees a 'welcome' task that explains what they are expected to do and how to use the system. Then there is an initial survey where the new person answers questions and the system adjusts to those choices. How it adjusts is determined by you.
  • Surveys and feedback: The first survey, which you edited to suit your organization, is just one of many that you can produce. In this way you can ask members for information, but also let them choose what they want to do. Every answer clicked can set off a number of reactions without needing your attention. Many routine clerical actions can be automated.
  • Tasks: You can create any number of tasks and assign them to various persons. A task can be a training course. Every task can have many steps. A task can be a to-do list or just a way to track how far a process has advanced. A task can even become a soft production line. Any step on a task can cause automated processes to run. What runs is under your control.
  • Automated workflows: The ability to have automated reactions to survey answers or to task step means that it is possible to create automated workflows. This connects tasks, surveys and other actions into organised processes.
  • Organisational structure: One of the other kinds of action that can be automated is the construction or dismantling of structures within your organization. Almost everything inside and outside the organization can be represented by a kind of name-tag (called an 'appro') inside the system. These appros can then be related to represent the structure of the organization. This means you can define relationships, teams, roles and permissions, and display the organisation's structure.
  • Recruitment: Every person in the system is represented by an appro. You can create referral links by attaching the appro and this means you can track recruitment and know who has recruited whom. As everything is an appro in the system you can also generate arbitrary referral links to represent anything. This makes simple A/B testing possible without extra software. It also makes it possible to track which social media produced a new recruit.
  • Internal messaging: Communicate with other members within the system. The internal messaging is also a place to keep notes as reminders to yourself. In addition it is possible to send messages to groups or even to a task or survey. Anything that is represented by an appro (which is virtually everything) can be sent a message. This creates emergent potential.
  • Payments: Accept fees and other payments through one of the integrated payment processors. Your organisation would have its own account with the payment processor. This means that payments from your members go to the payment processor and then into your bank.
  • Permissions: The system has 2 very strict permission systems to protect private data and to prevent unauthorised access to the database.
  • Context-aware help: The system has a single 'How?' button which opens a window with an explanation of whatever module you have open. Change the module and the information updates to the new module.

The system is categorized as an organisation operating system. This is a new category of software. It provides the foundational needs of almost every kind of organization. What it leaves the existing software companies to is adding apps to handle the differences of organizations. The concept is that every organization would have an organization operating system and on top of that would add apps for their specific needs such as an app to handle sports or an app for first-aid groups.

How Do You Get Started?

Whether you are starting with an idea, bringing people together around a common cause, or turning an existing audience into an active organisation, you can apply for your own independent system.

  1. Apply for your own independent system website. This is done on the HQ website.
  2. Establish your initial organisational structure and permissions.
  3. Publish your aims, plans and policies.
  4. Set up membership, recruitment and onboarding.
  5. Start assigning activities and collecting feedback.
  6. Develop your organisation as it grows.
